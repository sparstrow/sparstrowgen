# Product

## Register

product

## Users

sparstrowgen is for people who use coding-agent CLIs on their own Windows computers and want one
place to continue work across providers. The primary owner is a business systems analyst with
strong product judgment who works by seeing realistic designs, choosing a direction, and then
verifying the real behavior. Invited users should not need hosting, Git, or server knowledge.

## Product Purpose

Keep a provider-neutral conversation as the durable record while local coding agents do the work.
A person can change provider when availability or limits demand it without losing the conversation,
and can understand which computer, provider, and model will act before spending tokens.

## Brand Personality

Familiar, candid, quietly capable, and cost-aware. The product should feel like a serious working
tool that disappears behind the conversation, not a dashboard asking to be managed.

## Anti-references

- A provider cockpit where gauges and capacity graphics dominate the work.
- A generic SaaS dashboard assembled from equal cards and decorative metrics.
- Terminal cosplay that makes ordinary product actions look like command-line output.
- Interfaces that invent certainty, especially comparable limits or machine state the backend
  cannot actually know.
- Novel navigation that makes people learn a fourth interaction model to use three familiar agents.

## Design Principles

1. Keep the conversation primary. Provider, model, cost, and machine state support the work.
2. Show only what the system knows. Unknown, waiting, and unavailable are distinct honest states.
3. Prefer familiar interaction patterns, then spend novelty only where the product is genuinely new.
4. Put the consequence beside the action. Replay cost, disconnect impact, and update waiting belong
   where the person decides.
5. Preserve useful asymmetry. A person's prompt is compact context; the agent's answer is the
   reading surface.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use keyboard-operable standard controls, visible focus, semantic labels, non-color state cues, and
readable contrast at browser zoom and phone width. Motion must respect reduced-motion preference.
WCAG 2.2 AA is the implementation baseline unless a later approved requirement is stricter.
